# PROBLEM SOLVINGS FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES THINKING 
## A simple way to solve problems by reasoning from first principles.

## Background

The first-principles thinking or simply _“reasoning from first principles”_ is as true and effective for software engineer and other _technology enthusiasts/thought leaders_ as it is for all problem solvers. It even gets much easier if you are already a systems thinker, as **systems thinkings** opens up your mind to a systems perspective way of thinking when solving problems, coming up with ideas, and breaking down complex concepts.

It not only aligns with but is also similar to a technique, popularized by Richard Feynman for learning and solving problems. This **“Feynman Technique”** involves breaking down a complex concept or problem into its most basic parts, understanding it and then attempting to explain it in the simplest way possible often referred to as "layman's language", then identifying where your understanding breaks down.

The First Principles thinking approach is rooted in classic ancient Greek philosophy, particularly Aristotle’s teachings, emphasizes logic and essential truths rather than relying on analogy or precedent.

## What is First Principles thinking?

First Principles thinking, also referred to as _"Reasoning from first principles"_ is a powerful tool for problem-solving, that involves breaking down complex problems into their most fundamental components/truths _(or the very foundation that cannot be broken down any further)_, thereby enabling one to properly understand the root-cause, instead of relying on previous solutions, assumptions, analogies or biases, which then leads to build better thoughtful and innovative solutions from the ground up. 

Fundamental components/truths are the starting point from which all other knowledge in a given domain or on a system can be built. First principles thinker will relentlessly break down problems, ideas, challenges or even systems into these fundamental parts to enable them work through each part until they reach a conclusion, or root cause analysis that facilitates the building of a new approach/solution from the ground up.

By digging deeper into a problem, challenge or situation until we arrive at it's very foundation that cannot be broken down any further, we're forced to think outside the box through critically questioning the assumptions of each obstacle in order to get to the core of the problem thereby enabling us uncover key information that might have been skipped in the process of thinking by analogy, preconceived notions, cognitive biases, or assumptions based on past experiences when tackling complex challenges. 

Reasoning from first principles is probably one of the best ways to discover new solutions, as it involves breaking down ideas, challenges or problems into their basic elements _(fundamental truths)_ in order to better understand them which then facilitates reconstructing solutions from the ground up. 

### Breaking Down First principles thinking further

First principles thinking at its heart, is a way of analyzing complex problems by looking at their root causes and irreducible truths instead of thinking by analogy, preconceived notions, cognitive biases, or assumptions based on past experiences.  

Think of **Notions** as the ideas, opinions, or beliefs we form about how things work and **Biases** as the systematic mental errors or leaning tendencies that skew one's thinking away from objective reality. 

Then **Preconceived Notions** are usually the early assumptions or stereotypes about people, places, or topics that we hold before gathering all the facts.

**Cognitive Biases** are the predictable mental shortcuts or flaws in thinking that help the brain save energy but distort objective judgment. Most of the time, it's these _Preconceived notions_ and _cognitive biases_ that shape how we as humans process information, make decisions, and judge others. 

**Assumptions** are the beliefs or statements accepted as true without proof or direct evidence, e.g. unstated premises, logical foundations, and potential risks in reasoning.

**Thinking by analogy** is the cognitive process of transferring structural understanding from a familiar domain (source) to an unfamiliar domain (target), forming the bedrock of human learning, creativity, and problem-solving by recognizing deep relational similarities rather than surface-level differences.

By and large, First Principles thinking (_Reasoning from first principles_) boils down to deconstructing them to their core elements/fundamental truths to understand their underly root cause, then after rebuild solutions from the ground up.

In Software Engineering or even business engineering, first principles thinking would translate into questioning every assumption surrounding a challenge, problem, 'pain point' or situation, followed by understanding the 'why' behind each decision as a way of arriving at a clear Root Cause (Root Cause Analysis) and later coming up with good innovative solutions.

## Why First Principles Thinking matters in Software Engineering?

Software engineering often deals with intricate systems and rapidly changing technologies. As a software Engineer solving complex business and software related problems, you will constantly be required to stripe down the problems you encounter to their fundamental components, in order to find more effective and creative solutions, much like how Elon Musk redefined space and automotive industries by questioning, and reimagining their most basic assumptions to rebuild solutions from the group up.

**Applying first principles thinking allows engineers to:**

- Innovate Beyond Conventions: By not being confined to existing solutions, engineers can develop novel approaches.
- Enhance Problem-Solving Skills: Understanding the root causes leads to more effective and efficient solutions.
- Improve Decision-Making: Making choices based on fundamental truths rather than assumptions reduces errors.

With First Principles thinking, you essentially break down a challenge, idea, concept, topic, etc., to a fundamental truth that cannot be reduced/broken-down any further, then go on to construct a solution from the ground up. You are reasoning up from the first principles to simple, relevant, applicable and scalable solutions.

In order to build systems that are applicable, cost effective and scalable, your mindset should be questioning assumptions, pinpointing and understanding the constraints affecting these systems, identifying bottlenecks that need unblocking and how to unblock them, all down to their fundamental truths and facts that cannot be reduced any further so that you can reasoning upward from those fundamental truths to build solutions optimized for simplicity.

Approaching problem solving through the First principles thinking scope empowers software engineers, technology/business enthusiasts thought leaders, and all other problem solvers to operate beyond conventional problem-solving methods by focusing on fundamental truths, thereby fostering innovation, enhancing decision-making, eventually leading to more robust solutions when tackling the complex challenges in business,  software engineering and even our daily personal situations.

## How to Apply First Principles Thinking to problem solving: A Step-by-Step Guide

- **Identify the Problem:** Clearly identify, and define the issue, problem, challenge or situation you intend to understand or change without jumping to solutions. This also includes your current assumptions.
- **Break Down the Problem into it's findamental components/principles/truths:** Decompose or break down this problem into its fundamental components.
- **Challenge Assumptions:** Question and challenge the assumptions of these components, their relationships, existing beliefs and consider alternative perspectives.
- **Rebuild the solution from the Ground Up:** Construct solutions or understandings from the ground up based on the fundamental truths identified. 

## Challenges of First Principles Thinking

While powerful, this approach isn't without its challenges:

- Time-Consuming: Breaking down problems thoroughly requires significant time and effort.
- Requires Open-Mindedness: Being willing to question long-held beliefs can be uncomfortable.
- Risk of Overcomplication: There's a danger of getting lost in the details and losing sight of practical solutions.

## How to Apply First Principles Thinking in Your personal and professional situations.

- As we already know First principles thinking is every effective with Problem Solving, as it can help you tackle persistent personal/professional challenges or obstacles by identifying root causes through breaking them down to their fundamental truths/components thereby challenging assumptions about why things are the way they are to arrive at solutions.
- Further more, first principles thinking improves Decision Making by evaluating options based on fundamental needs, and actual desired outcomes, rather than just following notions, biases or popular opinions.
- You can use first Principles thinking to for personal growth. By consistently breaking down your challenges and successes in life, you begin to understand your own behaviors, beliefs, motivations, triggers, and turn offs by asking “Why?” repeatedly and challenging the assumptions, biases, and opinions you hold about yourself and the world.
- First principles thinking can enhance your learning by break down complex subjects or topics *(e.g., Systems engineering, Software engineering, project management, business analysis, product management or practical skills like Home DIY projects, sports etc)* into foundational concepts to understand the underlying rules and relationships, instead of trying to memorize facts (cramming).
- Innovation: Questioning the fundamental truths underneath the assumptions behind existing problems, ideas, or processes drive innovation in business as it facilitates the invention of completely new and more effective ways of doing things.

## Conclusion

First principles thinking involves breaking down complex problems into their most fundamental components/truths in order to understand them, and then reassemble them from scratch to properly understand root causes, instead of relying on biases, assumptions, analogies or previous solutions, encouraging a deep understanding of the problem's basic truths.

This approach encouraging a deep understanding of the problem's basic truths, because being able to identify the basic principles of a problem allows us to come up with innovative solutions.

Reasoning from first principles requires you to approach a problem/challenge by breaking it down to the most basic truths (first principles) and then rebuilding a solution from those fundamental truths/principles, ground up.
