## What is a System?
To properly break down systems thinking, First and foremost, we need to understand what a system is.
Think back as a child in a biology class, when we studied the respiratory system, the digestive system, the reproductive system and so forth. I’m sure you found it as interesting as I did.

Each of those systems has multiple parts, and together those parts work to accomplish a certain objective in the body. Further more when each of those systems are put together, they become the human body which is also a system of its own. 

And if we think even further, like looking around our environment then we shall see that a human is also part of a bigger systems like a family, a community, a town, a nation or a continent all the way up to a planet earth is a system.

With that in mind, we can now go ahead to define a system as a set of interconnected parts(components) working together as a whole, where a change ‘in’ or ‘removal of’ one can impact all the others. 

Systems can be found everywhere from the human body to the universe coming in various forms and complexities, and therefore being able to understanding them is key to navigating and understanding our complex world. 

## Types Of Systems

Systems can be quite complex and can extend to an infinite number of parts. 

Systems are categorized into several major types based on their structure, behavior, and purpose for example, we have closed systems, open systems, physical systems, abstract Systems, Natural systems, Artificial Systems, Deterministic and Probabilistic Systems to mention but a few.

- Closed systems are self contained and have limited interaction with the environment outside of them. a closed system is isolated from environmental influences. In reality completely closed systems are rare. Think of a self-sustaining miniature ecosystem, enclosed glass, or an R&D division working on a top-secret innovation. 

- Open systems, on the other hand, are more dynamic and interact with the external environment. An open system continually interacts with its environments. It receives inputs from and delivers output to the outside. Think of the supply chain department that interacts with external vendors, restaurants that deal with customers and other service providers, or a forest that exchange energy with the rest of the world around it. 

- Physical systems are tangible entities that may be static or dynamic in operation. 
- Abstract systems are conceptual or non-physical entities which may be as straightforward as formulas of relationships among sets of variables or models – the abstract conceptualization of physical situations.

- A deterministic system is one in which the occurrence of all events is perfectly predictable because it operates in a predictable way where a specific input always produces the exact same output.

- A Probabilistic system is one in which the occurrence of events cannot be perfectly predicted _(their future states or outcomes cannot be predicted with 100% certainty)_, often involving alot of uncertainty and randomness.

## What is Systems Thinking?
Inside each of these systems are many interactions and patterns that form. Let's take an example, of a typical [Café Javas](https://cafejavas.co.ug/user/home) restaurant and coffee shop chain in [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda).

If you live in [Uganda](https://utb.go.ug/) then you know [Café Javas](https://cafejavas.co.ug/user/home) has multiple popular branches and outlets across [Uganda](https://exploreuganda.com/), with each serving a cluster of its wide clientele depending on their location. Some of the clients will often switch between the branches depending on where the are at the particular moment, which is not their home.

This restaurant takes in customers, ingredients/supplies, and money from one side and releases food, drinks, and happy customers on the other side. Therefore, if this restaurant is to consistently function at its very best, it’s many subsystems, such as the kitchen, dining area, coffee stands, menu POS units, ushers, cashiers, the manager on duty, etc, must all work together to serve the customer.

We also need to remember that this restaurant doesn’t exist or operate in isolation. It interacts with external entities like suppliers for the ingredients, payment services to take transactions, marketing agencies to promote the restaurant, and the government for tax payments among other entities which are all sub-systems of a bigger complex system that is also a sub-system of another system.

All of these are connected and a change in one will impact just about everything. For example, an increase in marketing might increase the flow of customers, which will impact how the restaurant deals with its suppliers. 

A disruption in the restaurant’s supply chain can impact the number of customers they're able to serve, which can impact the restaurant’s revenue and profits which can also impact payroll, which can also impact the attitude of the staff. 

A change in the dining area can impact the customer's flow, impacting the kitchen and supply. 

A change in the regulations, policies or taxes can impact the restaurant in different ways which is why most business people keenly conduct [SWOT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M-D6DbJswR4) & [PESTLE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=noSF-R1JfGk) analyses whenever possible to make sure they are still on the right business track. Therefore, the owners of this restaurant, must constantly watch these interacting elements internally & externally to maintain smooth operation. 

This is exactly what [systems thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_thinking) is all about. It’s important to know that this topic is broad and can require a lot of research, learning and understanding depending on your area of expertise or what you require systems thinking for. But at the heart of it all, understanding how systems work broadens our perspectives on problem solving, generating ideas and creative innovation among other things.

This is exactly what systems thinking is all about. A mindset that looks at the bigger picture and considers how different parts of a system interact and influence each other. 

It is basically the idea that everything is connected, and that a change in one part of the system can have effects on the whole system which helps us understand the web of interaction that governs our environment, our society, and our businesses. 

Most importantly, it helps us find ways to influence that web of interactions for the better. In conclusion, we have taken our first step to explore the world of systems thinking. 

## Why Systems Thinking?

[Systems thinking](https://thesystemsthinker.com/systems-thinking-what-why-when-where-and-how/) is important, simply because it helps us make better decisions. It forces us to look beyond the immediate and obvious and see the bigger picture. By doing so, we can avoid the pitfalls of short-sighted decisions that might save time, money, and resources in the short term, but could lead to unfavourable outcomes in the long run.

[System thinking](https://thesystemsthinker.com/systems-thinking-what-why-when-where-and-how/) can help us identify the root causes of the problem and help us develop more effective and sustainable solutions that addresses the underlying issues rather than just providing temporary solutions. This is even more effective if we apply [First Principles thinking](https://jamesclear.com/first-principles) approach to our [systems thinking](https://thesystemsthinker.com/systems-thinking-what-why-when-where-and-how/).

Understanding Systems thinking is simply knowing how everything from our bodies, our cities, form different systems that work together. The theory of [Systems thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_thinking) provides us a way of tackling those big, messy, real world problems, especially the ones that threaten the survival of our society, among other things.
